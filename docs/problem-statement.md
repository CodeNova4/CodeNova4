# Problem Statement
Military organisations cannot reliably determine whether aircraft, vehicles, and equipment are
mission-ready.
Maintenance runs on fixed calendar schedules, regardless of actual component
condition.
• HUMS (Health & Usage Monitoring System) sensor data that could predict failures
weeks in advance sits unanalysed.
When platforms fail unexpectedly, operational readiness drops and recovery takes
weeks.
The US military spends $90B/year on maintenance — shifting to predictive approaches
saves billions

## Background
Military equipment needs high reliability and mission readiness. Traditional fixed-schedule maintenance can miss early signs of failure. Our solution uses sensor and maintenance data to predict failures, identify non-ready assets, and prioritise maintenance, improving operational readiness and reducing unexpected breakdowns.



## The Problem
Military organisations cannot accurately determine the readiness of their equipment. Fixed maintenance schedules and unused sensor data can lead to unexpected failures, higher costs, and reduced mission readiness.



## Who is Affected

Maintenance officers and crews, who lack a single, trustworthy view offleet readiness.
Mission planners, who cannot confirm an asset will still be operational by its next
mission window.
Command staff, who absorb the cost and operational risk of unplanned downtime.

## Why It Matters

Unexpected equipment failures can reduce mission readiness, increase maintenance costs, and cause operational delays. Predictive maintenance helps detect problems early, prevent breakdowns, and ensure critical equipment is ready when needed.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Existing maintenance systems mainly rely on fixed schedules and manual analysis. They often fail to use sensor data effectively, making it difficult to detect early failures, predict breakdowns, and prioritise maintenance.
